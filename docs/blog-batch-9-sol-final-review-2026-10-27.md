## Overall finding

Both bilingual articles pass the final scientific and editorial audit. Every quoted monthly value, eligible-month calendar, best-month list and representative-cell elevation reproduces the current public records. The interpretations stay within the scope of 35-year selected-cell averages and do not turn those values into forecasts, route-opening calendars or trail-safety claims.

The articles answer different search questions with different editorial structures. The South Africa article is organised around the country's west-to-east rainfall reversal; the Morocco article is organised around the elevation-driven winter-to-summer trade-off. Both provide practical decisions, contextual links and explicit model limitations. Their lead images are licensed, attributed and 2,400 × 1,600 pixels.

## South Africa review

### Claims verified

- Table Mountain, Drakensberg and Blyde River are recommendation-eligible in all twelve months; Cederberg is withheld in January and February because of the critical heat rule.
- March through December is the exact common eligible window across the four records.
- The January contrast is reproduced: 8.48 percent wet days at Cederberg versus 85.35 percent at Drakensberg, while Cederberg's 66.54 percent hot-day probability triggers the score cap of 49.
- June values reproduce the public temperature, wet-day, precipitation and snow records: the Cape records are cooler and wetter, Drakensberg is cold and comparatively dry, and Blyde River is warm and very dry.
- Drakensberg's June 10th-to-90th temperature range is 2.2–13.6 °C and its modelled snow-day probability is 9.52 percent.
- The September-to-October rainfall increase at Drakensberg, from 65.0 to 153.6 mm, reproduces the source record.

### Interpretation verified

The article correctly rejects one national hiking month and explains the seasonal rainfall reversal without claiming route-level weather. April is presented as a broad climatic overlap, June as an eastern dry-season option and September as a better western-mountain compromise. Current wind, cloud, fire, snow, river, permit and access conditions remain outside the model claim.

## Morocco review

### Claims verified

- Atlas Mountains and M'Goun are recommendation-eligible in all twelve months. Anti-Atlas is eligible January–May and September–December; Rif is eligible January–June and September–December.
- January through May and September through December are the exact common eligible windows.
- July values reproduce the elevation split: Atlas and M'Goun average about 22 °C and pass, while Anti-Atlas and Rif fail the heat gate at 94.10 and 67.10 percent hot days.
- April values reproduce all four public records, including 6.76 and 9.43 percent modelled snow days in the high cells and the wetter Rif record.
- In November the Anti-Atlas reaches score 91 at 15.1 °C, while the two high cells fall below 8 °C and their modelled snow-day probabilities rise to 12.86 and 20.95 percent.

### Interpretation verified

The article correctly makes mountain range and elevation part of the month decision. It presents April as the widest balanced overlap, summer as a move to higher cells and late autumn as a lower-range option. It explicitly excludes pass access, snow depth, flash floods, water, refuge dates, route finding and exposed-ridge gusts from the monthly evidence.
