# BestTimeToHike blog editorial plan

Status: editorial blueprint for implementation  
Evidence base: the 94 quality-gated destination articles and their published 1991–2025 historical monthly metrics
Languages: English and German

## Editorial promise

The blog answers practical hiking-planning questions with BestTimeToHike's own versioned data. It is not a collection of destination pages with new introductions, and it is not a programmatic SEO expansion.

Every article must have its own question, argument, evidence selection, visual rhythm and ending. Shared site furniture is allowed; a shared article template is not. The technical content model should therefore provide composable blocks rather than a mandatory sequence of hero, three headings, FAQ and call to action.

## Scientific boundaries

- Describe the project-defined historical period 1991–2025, never a forecast or a guarantee for a particular trip.
- Attribute every numeric claim to the published BestTimeToHike dataset version.
- Use temperature during sampled hiking hours, wet-day probability, snow-day probability, hot-day probability, daylight and the selected representative elevation/cell only with their published definitions.
- Do not use unvalidated ERA5-Land grid wind to recommend, rank or characterise a destination.
- Do not convert a representative climate cell into a claim about every trail, summit, closure or hazard in a region.
- Treat a recommendation-eligible month as a model result, not as proof that every route is open or safe.
- Link readers to the methodology and current local trail/weather information where the decision requires it.

## No-rigid-pattern rule

The first six posts must use six different editorial shapes:

1. myth-versus-data investigation;
2. paired seasonal-calendar comparison;
3. hemisphere-based field guide;
4. daylight timeline and latitude comparison;
5. data-literacy explainer built around matched examples;
6. twelve-part annual planning atlas.

No two posts may share the same ordered block signature. Reusable blocks may include prose, annotated metrics, a comparison table, month strip, timeline, map, pull quote, caveat, destination cards and related links, but each article chooses only the blocks its argument needs.

## First editorial batch

### 1. Dry does not always mean good hiking weather

- **English slug:** `dry-does-not-mean-hikeable`
- **German working title:** `Trocken heißt nicht automatisch gutes Wanderwetter`
- **Question:** Why can an exceptionally dry month still be a poor hiking choice?
- **Shape:** open with the apparent contradiction, show three annotated counterexamples, explain the heat gate, then contrast them with cooler shoulder months.
- **Primary evidence:** Wadi Rum in July has a 0.0% historical wet-day share, a 30.0 °C hiking-hour mean and a 99.68% hot-day probability; Arches in July has 8.6% wet days, 30.7 °C and 97.1% hot days; Mallorca in July has 5.7% wet days, 27.5 °C and 78.82% hot days. All three months are withheld by the recommendation gate.
- **Useful contrast:** Wadi Rum in March averages 17.4 °C with 5.05% wet days; November averages 18.3 °C with 2.22% wet days.
- **Distinct visual:** a dry-to-hot contradiction plot, not a ranked list.

### 2. Twelve months or three: how wide is a hiking window?

- **English slug:** `wide-and-narrow-hiking-seasons`
- **German working title:** `Zwölf Monate oder drei: Wie groß ist das Wanderfenster?`
- **Question:** Why do some destinations have a broad modelled season while others have only a short window?
- **Shape:** split-screen year strips, followed by four short destination stories and a section on what the model cannot say about trail access.
- **Broad-window evidence:** San Pedro de Atacama, Tenerife, Table Mountain and Madeira each have 12 recommendation-eligible months in the current selected set.
- **Narrow-window evidence:** Grand Teton, Cerro Castillo, Jasper, Chamonix, Grindelwald, Landmannalaugar and Jotunheimen each have three recommendation-eligible months.
- **Distinct visual:** two opposing twelve-cell calendars with reasons attached to the closed months.

### 3. Shoulder-season hiking across both hemispheres

- **English slug:** `shoulder-season-hiking-worldwide`
- **German working title:** `Wandern in der Nebensaison auf beiden Hemisphären`
- **Question:** Where do spring and autumn produce useful combinations of temperature, rain frequency and daylight?
- **Shape:** north/south hemisphere field guide rather than a global ranking; organise the article around travel decisions and transitions between seasons.
- **Evidence set:** Tenerife in May (15.9 °C, 5.27% wet days, 13.6 h daylight), Samaria Gorge in May (17.4 °C, 16.13%, 14.0 h), Elqui Valley in March (16.5 °C, 14.84%, 12.2 h), Sequoia and Kings Canyon in September (15.3 °C, 18.56%, 12.4 h), and San Pedro de Atacama in October (19.4 °C, 0.43%, 12.6 h).
- **Distinct visual:** a two-hemisphere seasonal path with destination notes, not score cards.

### 4. When daylight changes the hiking decision

- **English slug:** `daylight-and-hiking-season`
- **German working title:** `Wenn Tageslicht die Wanderentscheidung verändert`
- **Question:** How much does daylight vary between high-latitude and near-equatorial hiking destinations?
- **Shape:** a solar timeline from winter minimum to summer maximum, then three paired latitude comparisons.
- **High-variation evidence:** Lofotodden ranges from 0.4 to 24.0 hours, Landmannalaugar from 4.4 to 20.7, and Denali from 4.6 to 20.5.
- **Stable comparison:** Rwenzori and Mount Kenya remain at about 12.1 hours; Cotopaxi ranges only from 12.1 to 12.2.
- **Distinct visual:** horizontal daylight arcs; do not present daylight as sunshine duration or safe walking time.

### 5. Rainfall total and rainy-day frequency answer different questions

- **English slug:** `rainfall-total-versus-wet-days`
- **German working title:** `Regenmenge und Regentage sagen nicht dasselbe aus`
- **Question:** Why can two months with nearly identical rainfall totals feel very different for trip planning?
- **Shape:** matched-pair evidence lab, with definitions placed next to the examples instead of a generic methodology section.
- **Primary pair:** Salkantay in September and Cederberg in June both average 84.2 mm, but their wet-day shares are 86.44% and 27.44% respectively.
- **Secondary pair:** Salkantay in September (84.2 mm, 86.44%) versus Jeju in October (85.7 mm, 23.66%).
- **Distinct visual:** equal-volume vessels beside unequal calendar-dot distributions.

### 6. A year of mild hiking: twelve months, twelve different places

- **English slug:** `mild-hiking-around-the-year`
- **German working title:** `Ein Jahr mit mildem Wanderklima`
- **Question:** Can a traveller follow historically mild hiking-hour temperatures around the world throughout the year?
- **Shape:** a twelve-stop annual atlas, one concise decision card per month, with a final explanation of the explicit selection rules.
- **Selection rule:** recommendation-eligible, 12–20 °C hiking-hour mean and no more than 25% wet days; select a different destination for every month.
- **Evidence itinerary:** January Elqui Valley, February Wadi Rum, March San Pedro de Atacama, April Tenerife, May Samaria Gorge, June Madeira, July Larapinta Trail, August Mount Rainier, September Sequoia and Kings Canyon, October Table Mountain, November Anti-Atlas, December Doi Inthanon.
- **Distinct visual:** calendar atlas; it must not be styled as a universal top-12 ranking.

## Publication quality gate

A post can be indexed only when all of the following pass:

- 700–1,200 substantive words per locale, excluding navigation and disclosures;
- a unique thesis and a block signature not already used by another post;
- at least four checked claims drawn from the versioned local dataset;
- visible explanation of the relevant metric definitions and limitations;
- at least three contextual destination links, one methodology link and one useful finder or ranking link;
- unique title, description, H1, canonical and reciprocal `hreflang`;
- licensed hero image with source, author, licence and useful alternative text;
- `BlogPosting` and breadcrumb structured data matching visible content;
- English and German editorial review, not literal string substitution;
- no unsupported current-weather, route-opening, trail-safety or wind claim;
- visual and accessibility checks at phone, tablet and desktop widths.

Drafts and failed posts remain `noindex` and outside the sitemap. The blog index itself becomes indexable only when at least three approved posts exist, so it is never an empty or thin archive.

## Technical handoff for Luna

Build a flexible block-based article system rather than one article component with fixed slots. The schema needs shared metadata plus an ordered union of optional block types. It must reject duplicate block signatures across this first batch and expose a data-evidence manifest so tests can verify every numeric claim against the published JSON. Add blog routes and navigation, but keep all blog URLs `noindex` until real bilingual articles pass the gate. Do not add the planned posts to the sitemap during scaffolding.
